### 4. Money rules

- **Stored as integer cents.** Bill text becomes cents by string handling, with no `parseFloat` and no `× 100`. The tip rate is integer tenths of a percent (12.5% is 125). People is an integer.
- **Input format.**
  - Bill: digits, optional `.`, at most 2 decimals.
  - Tip: at most 1 decimal.
  - People: digits only.
  - No commas, signs, inner spaces or exponent. Outer spaces are trimmed.
  - Too many decimals gives a message, never silent rounding.
  - Forms like `.5` and `5.` get decided in the criteria, and you will see them.
- **One rounding step.** The tip rounds half-up to the cent: `tip = floor((bill_cents × tenths + 500) / 1000)`. Example: bill 0.30 at 15% is exactly 0.045, so the tip is 0.05. Verified in Node: the formula gives 5 cents and float `toFixed(2)` gives 0.04. Total = bill + tip, exact.
- **Uneven split.**
  - `base = floor(total / people)` and `r = total mod people`.
  - The first `r` people pay `base + 1` cent and the rest pay `base`, so shares always add to the total exactly.
  - Verified example: 100.00 at 0% over 3 people gives base 3333 cents and r 1. One person pays 33.34 and two pay 33.33.
  - The page shows one line: "each pays X" when `r` is 0, otherwise "r people pay A, the other n−r pay B". People are unnamed, so who pays the extra cent does not matter.
  - I rejected rounding every share up (the group overpays) or down (the group underpays).
- **Tiny totals.** Zero-cent shares are allowed. Total 0.01 over 3 people means one pays 0.01 and two pay 0.00.
- **Output.** Two decimals, digits and one `.` only. No symbol and no thousands separators (`1000000.00`). Built from integers by string handling.

| Field | Allowed | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Bill | 0.01 to 1000000.00 | A zero bill is pointless and gets a message. The cap rejects typos and keeps integer math far below 2^53 (largest product is 10^8 cents × 1000 = 10^11). |
| Tip % | 0 to 100, one decimal | 0 means no tip. Above 100 is almost always a typo. |
| People | 1 to 100, whole number | 0 is the only divide-by-zero case and gets a message. |

```js
// For safe integers a % b is exact, so this division never rounds.
function quotient(a, b) {
  return (a - (a % b)) / b;
}

// The first `extra` shares are one cent larger than the rest.
function splitTotal(totalCents, people) {
  const peopleOk = Number.isInteger(people) && people >= LIMITS.peopleMin && people <= LIMITS.peopleMax;
  if (!isCount(totalCents) || !peopleOk) {
    return null;
  }
  const extra = totalCents % people;
  const base = quotient(totalCents, people);
  const shares = [];
  for (let i = 0; i < people; i += 1) {
    shares.push(i < extra ? base + 1 : base);
  }
  return { shares, extra };
}
```

**In plain words.** The function takes the total in whole cents and the number of people. It gives everyone the same base amount. It then hands the leftover cents out one each to the first people.

1. **Check the inputs.** The total must be a whole, non-negative number of cents. People must be a whole number from 1 to 50. Anything else returns `null`, never `NaN`, and it never throws.
2. **`extra = totalCents % people`.** This is what is left over after dividing as evenly as possible. It is always between 0 and people − 1.
3. **`base = quotient(totalCents, people)`.** This is the whole cents each person gets before the leftovers. `quotient` first subtracts the remainder, so what it divides is an exact multiple of `people`. The answer is a whole number with no rounding. `Math.floor(a / b)` would go through a decimal division instead.
4. **Build the list.** The first `extra` people get `base + 1` and everyone else gets `base`.

**Why the shares always add up exactly.** Division with a remainder means `total = base × people + extra`. Add the shares:

`extra × (base + 1) + (people − extra) × base = people × base + extra = total`

Every step is whole-number arithmetic, so nothing is rounded and no cent can be lost or gained. Because `extra` is always smaller than `people`, nobody pays more than 1 cent above anyone else. The larger shares come first.

**Examples:**
- **100.00 over 3 people.** Total 10000, base 3333, extra 1. The shares are 3334 + 3333 + 3333 = 10000, which is 33.34, 33.33 and 33.33.
- **0.49 over 50 people.** Base 0, extra 49. The shares are 49 × 1 + 1 × 0 = 49, so 49 people pay 0.01 and one pays 0.00.
- **115.00 over 2 people.** Base 5750, extra 0. The shares are 5750 + 5750, with no note.

- **Documented numbers.** S-3.1 to S-3.5 and S-3.7 check them.

# Tip and Bill Splitter

A web page that splits a restaurant bill equally, adds a tip and shows what each person owes. It is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript: no build step, no dependencies and no network use. This is a take-home assignment for a Product Engineering role.

## What it is, who it is for, and why

You enter the bill, a tip percentage (15 to start) and the number of people (2 to start). As you type, the page shows the tip, the total and what each person pays. When the total cannot be split into equal cents, it says how the extra cents are shared. Reset starts again.

It is for one role, the Table Payer: a person at a restaurant table who settles the whole bill and then has to collect each person's share. The group has agreed to split equally, and any shortfall comes out of the payer's own pocket ([docs/app-roles.md](docs/app-roles.md)).

I chose it because the person, the jobs and the one thing that must never happen are small and clear enough to write down and test. I wanted a small app that fully works and is well specified, not a big one that does not. The jobs ([docs/jobs-to-be-done.md](docs/jobs-to-be-done.md)) are:

- J-1 Tell everyone what they owe.
- J-2 Settle on a tip.
- J-3 Share out the odd cents.
- J-4 Redo the sums when things change.
- J-5 Pay in cash without waiting for change. Not served in this version.

The one thing that must never happen is showing a wrong or out-of-date amount: shares that do not add up exactly to the total, or an old result still on screen after an entry became unusable.

## Assumptions

- **The number of people includes the payer**, who also owes a share. The page does not say so.
- **The bill is the amount on the check**, with any tax or service charge already in it. The tip is added on top. There is no separate tax entry.
- **The tip is worked out on the bill as entered**: the percentage times the bill, rounded half-up to the cent, once. The total is the bill plus the tip. For example, a bill of 1.05 at 10% is 0.105, which shows as 0.11. A calculator that uses floating point can show 0.10. The page does not say it rounds half-up.
- **Equal split only.** When the total does not divide evenly, the leftover cents go one each to the first people, meaning the first places in the numbered list. People are not named, so who gets the extra cent does not matter. The result says so, and the shares add up exactly to the total.
- **Currency-neutral display.** Amounts have two decimals, with no currency symbol and no thousands separators, so what you read is what you can type, for example `2000000.00`. This assumes a currency with two decimal places.
- **Limits.** Bill 0.01 to 1000000.00. Tip 0 to 100, with up to two decimals. People 1 to 50, as a whole number. Anything else gets a message and no result. A bill of 0.01 split three ways is allowed and shows 0.01, 0.00 and 0.00.
- **Input rules.**
  - Bill and Tip %: digits and at most one decimal point, up to two decimals. ".5" and "5." are fine. "." on its own is not.
  - People: digits only, so "2.5", "2.0" and "2." are rejected.
  - Leading zeros are fine: "007" is 7.
  - Spaces, tabs and non-breaking spaces at the ends are ignored. Other blank characters, and any blank inside a number, are rejected.
  - Commas, signs, exponents, letters and digits from other scripts are rejected.
  - Only a decimal point is accepted as the decimal mark. I have not checked whether a phone keypad in a region that uses a comma offers a point.
  - If a value has too many decimals and is also out of range, the message about decimals is shown.
  - An empty Bill is not an error: there is just no result yet. An empty Tip % or People gets its own message.
- **Messages follow every keystroke**, so a message can show briefly while a number is half typed. For example, typing 0 on the way to 0.50 shows "The bill must be at least 0.01." until the next digit.
- **English only.** The page stores nothing and sends nothing.
